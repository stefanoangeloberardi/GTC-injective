This paper considers a variant of the global trace condition (GTC) for
cyclic proofs, where the trace relation is injective, and shows that
GTC corresponds to the size change termination (SCT) of fan-in-free
SCT graphs. This result may be viewed as a refinement of the recent
result of Ikebuchi [6], where the injectivity condition was not taken
into account.

compared with [6], the only new contribution is to observe that the
trace relation can be made injective, so that GCT is equivalent to SCT
for fan-in-free graphs.

SCORE: 2 (accept)
----- TEXT:
This paper is a continuation of [11]. First, the authors introduce the notion of transition graphs, an abstraction of size-change graphs, and define the transition graph associated with a CLKID^\omega pre-proof in the way that the graph satisfies SCT condition iff the pre-proof is a proof, following [11]. Then, they show that the transition graph of a pre-proof is injective and that for an injective transition graph, SCT is equivalent to the Progress Domain Condition (PDC) -- any path v -->* v has a progressing trace.

Overall, the paper is well-written and shows an interesting new result on size-change graphs of a cyclic proof. The property that a transition graph is injective, or in traditional words, a size-change graph is fan-in free, is great since in that case the worst-case size of the ranking function of the graph is singly exponential while it is triply exponential in general. It affects the complexity of the proof with induction transformed from a cyclic proof.
The progress domain condition introduced in the paper is interesting. The paper does not establish the “benefit” of it but I think that this condition should be known to the community and studied further.
The proof of the main theorem is correct as far as I can see. Also, the paper is indeed in the scope of CSL. 
Therefore, I am in favor of its acceptance.

----- TEXT:
The given paper is about termination criteria for cyclic proofs.
Here, a cyclic proof is a regular sequent calculus proof in the shape of
a derivation tree with one deviation: instead of applying an inference rule to prove
a certain sequent, one can also state, that this sequent already occurred
somewhere else in the proof, leading to cycles in the proof.
This is known as a pre-proof.

To finalize a pre-proof, it is required that every loop in the cyclic proof 
corresponds to a strict decrease w.r.t. some well-founded relation, so that
essentially well-founded induction will turn all cycles into applications
of some induction hypothesis. To this end, sizes of arguments of sequents 
are approximated by finite labeled graphs (the labels encode a strict 
or a weak decrease of arguments), and a global trace condition (GTC) 
- a termination criterion for the abstract graphs - ensures the existence
of the required well-founded relation. 

In existing work [11] the connection between cyclic proofs, GTC and size-change
termination (SCT) is shown. The approximation of cyclic proofs by finite labeled graphs
for a similar proof system is also known [6,5,8].

In my view the core contributions to this paper are:

(A) Unlike to [6,5,8], here the translation will produce *injective* abstract graphs.

(B) For injective abstract graphs, SCT can be decided by a more simple criterion than
  the original one. This new criterion is introduced as Progress Domain Condition (PDC).
  
Unfortunately, I have some doubts about the significance of these contributions for 
the following reasons. 

Concerning (B): although (PDC) is simpler than the original SCT-criterion, 
  they are both EXPSPACE algorithms, where in particular in both algorithms
  the full transitive closure of the graphs need to be computed. 
  Here, I would have expected at least some experiments that show the improved
  performance of PDC over SCT on some set of benchmark problems.
  
  Is there any realistic example where the SCT criterion is too slow, but PDC
  proves termination within a reasonable time limit? 
  
  
Concerning (A): Comparing the translation to injective graphs in this paper with
  the one of [6,5,8], it seems to me that the existing one is better than the
  new one, since the former allows to get more relationships in the abstract graphs, 
  e.g., when exploiting equality. And more relationships means that it might 
  be the case that GTC and SCT is satisfied more often. 
  In this way, restricting to the injective case just feels wrong. 

  For instance, given a cyclic pre-proof such as the one in Figure 2, why shouldn't
  one add further blue and red arrows, if (by coincidence), two arguments of 
  a sequent are identical, or if they can be shown to be equal by using equality
  as indicated in Remark 28? Just in order to be able to apply the PDC for 
  the injective case? Then this needs to justified by experiments, that this
  gives some kind of benefit over simply applying the original SCT algorithm.
  

For these reasons, I rate the paper as borderline: the new PDC algorithm might
be interesting, but its significance remains unclear.
