\section{Related Work and Conclusions}
\label{sec:Related}

The sequent calculus with inductive definitions in Section \ref{sec:cyclic} is a variant of the systems in \cite{Brotherston1,Brotherston2} and \cite{BrotherstonSimpson}, to which it is equivalent. \marginpar{Stefano integra questa parte} 
In the literature, the global trace condition is defined in terms of infinitely progressing traces or as infinite descending sequences. They are both instances of Fermat's method of infinite descent, which is used to argue by contradiction that a cyclic pre-proof satisfying the GTC is semantically valid. It is known that valid cyclic proofs correspond to finite proofs with induction only if the system includes arithmetic \cite{Berardi}.

The transition graphs are a variant of size-change graphs in \cite{Lee1,Lee2,Lee3}; in particular, injective transition graphs correspond to fan-in free size-change graphs
as defined in \cite[\S 2.4]{Lee3}. Size-change graphs are a technique for verifying program termination by building suitable abstractions of functional programs so that the SCT condition implies termination. In \cite{Mirai}, it is shown that, up to an appropriate association of a set of size-change graphs such that the composition determines the paths in the proof, the GTC and SCT conditions are equivalent, see there, Proposition 2. This result is rephrased in our Proposition \ref{prop:GTC-SCT}. 

The Progress Domain Condition, presented here in Section \ref{sec:progressDomainCond}, is new in our knowledge. It is decidable by the finiteness of the bi-partite relations involved in a transition graph, and it is general enough, as we have shown in Theorem \ref{thr:injective-pre-proofs}, that the transition graphs associated with the pre-proofs in our version of the systems $\CLJIDomega$ and $\CLKIDomega$ are injective.

From  \cite{Lee3}, we know that deciding whether a set of fan-in free size-change graphs satisfies the SCT can be decided within time which is exponential in $n$, where $n$ essentially depends on the size of the set of variables of the analyzed program (more precisely on the size of ranking functions), which corresponds to the set $X$ of symbols in our writing. A brute force algorithm deciding PDC is also exponential, but we do not know whether restricting the problem to injective transition graphs associated with pre-proofs in our system can lead to better algorithms.
