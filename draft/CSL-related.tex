\section{Related Work and Conclusions}
\label{sec:Related}

The sequent calculus with inductive definitions in Section \ref{sec:cyclic} is a variant of the systems in \cite{Brotherston1,Brotherston2} and \cite{BrotherstonSimpson}, to which it can be shown to be equivalent in the case the system includes Peano Arithmetic.
We omit such a proof in this paper for space reasons. %\marginpar{Stefano integra questa parte} 
In the literature, the global trace condition is defined in terms of infinitely progressing traces or as infinite descending sequences. They are both instances of Fermat's method of infinite descent, which is used to argue by contradiction that a cyclic pre-proof satisfying the GTC is semantically valid. It is known that valid cyclic proofs correspond to \emph{finite} proofs with induction if the system includes arithmetic, while in some other cases they prove more theorems \cite{Berardi}.

The transition graphs are a variant of size-change graphs in \cite{Lee1,Lee2,Lee3}; in particular, injective transition graphs correspond to fan-in free size-change graphs
as defined in \cite[\S 2.4]{Lee3}. Size-change graphs are a technique for verifying program termination by building suitable abstractions of functional programs so that the SCT condition implies termination. 

In Ikebuchi's \cite{Mirai}, it is shown that, up to an appropriate association of a set of size-change graphs such that the composition determines the paths in the proof, the GTC and SCT conditions are equivalent, see there, Proposition 2. This result is rephrased for Transition Graphs 
in our Proposition \ref{prop:GTC-SCT}. 

The Progress Domain Condition, presented here in Section \ref{sec:progressDomainCond}, is new in our knowledge. It is decidable by the finiteness of the bi-partite relations involved in a transition graph, and it is general enough, as we have shown in Theorem \ref{thr:injective-pre-proofs},
in the case that the transition graphs associated with %the pre-proofs in our version of the systems 
$\CLJIDomega$ and $\CLKIDomega$ are injective.

The proof of Theorem \ref{thr:PDC+injImpliesGTC} is non-constructive since it is based on infinite Ramsey's Theorem, which is a purely classical result. However, we know
of a constructive proof of this theorem, which we have omitted for the sake of brevity. We understand Theorem \ref{thr:PDC+injImpliesGTC} and its constructive proof as a step toward efficient translations of cyclic proofs into ordinary inductive proofs, with an intuitionistic
translation in the case of intuitionistic cyclic proofs. These translations, in turn, are motivated
by cross-checking formal cyclic proofs using proof assistants that do not support cyclic proofs.
For the moment, such translations exist only in theory and are built over the
results of \cite{Berardi} and \cite{Mirai}.

In \cite{Lee1}, it is proved that the SCT problem in the general case is PSPACE-complete. 
From  \cite{Lee3}, we know that restricting to fan-in free size-change graphs, a lower bound of SCT is exponential in $n$, where $n$ essentially depends on the size of the set of formal paremeters of the analyzed program (more precisely on the size of expressions defining ranking functions), which corresponds to the set of the occurrences of inductive predicates in a cyclic pre-proof. A brute force algorithm deciding PDC is also exponential, but the relation between the complexity of PDC and the lower bound of SCT based on the size of ranking functions is unclear and deserves further study.
