\section{Transition Graphs and Size-Change Termination}
\label{sec:size-change}


Inspired by the work by Ikebuchi \cite{Mirai}, in this section we introduce the notion of {\em transition graphs}, which is intended to charecterize the global trace condition from the theory of cyclic proofs in terms of the {\em size-change termination} condition. We consider directed graphs whose edges are labelled with pairs of relations, that we call bi-partite relations. The properties relevant for termination can now be expressed in terms of compositions of such relations.
 
 \subsection{Bi-partite Relations and Transition Graphs}
 
Let $R \subseteq X \times Y$ be a binary relation, then we write $x R y$ for $(x,y) \in R$. 
Thinking of $R$ as a function $R: X \to \wp(Y)$, we set $R(x) = \Set{y \in Y \mid x R y}$. 
Then we define $\dom{R} = \Set{x \in X \mid \exists y \in Y.\; xRy} 
= \Set{x \in X \mid R(x) \neq \emptyset}$, and
$\cod{R} = \Set{y \in Y \mid \exists x \in X.\; xRy} = \bigcup_{x \in X} R(x)$. 

In this work, we shall consider binary relations over some fixed finite set 
$X = \Set{x_1, ..., x_n}$ of abstract ``paremeters'', which are consequently themselves finite. We say that $(S,P)$ is a {\em bi-partite} relation over $X$ if both $S, P \subseteq X \times X$ and $S \cap P = \emptyset$, namely if $S, P$ are a partition of the binary relation $S \cup P$ over $X$, with possibly $S$ or $P$ empty.

\begin{definition}[Transition graph]\label{def:transitionGraph}
Let $\biRel$ be a finite set of bi-partite relations over $X$.
A {\em transition graph} of $\biRel$ is a tuple $\Graph = (V, \biRel, E)$ where $V$ is a finite set of vertices, and $E \subseteq V \times \biRel\times V$ is a (finite) set of labeled edges.
\end{definition}

Let $\Graph = (V, \biRel, E)$; we abbreviate $(u, (S,P), v) \in E$ by $\edgeG{(S,P)}{u}{v}{\Graph}$.
Also, if $e = \edgeG{(S,P)}{u}{v}{\Graph}$ is any edge, 
then $\source{e} = u$, $\target{e} = v$, and $\lbl{e} = (S,P)$ are the {\em source}, {\em target}, and {\em label}
of $e$ respectively. When $\Graph$ is understood, we omit the subscripts in $\edge{(S,P)}{u}{v}$. 

Suppose that $\edgeG{(S,P)}{u}{v}{\Graph}$, and $x, y \in X$. When $x S y$ we say that the size associated with $x$ in the abstract state $u$ is equal or greater than the size associated with $y$ in $v$ in some intended ordering; hence, if $x S y$ we say that 
$x,y$ are {\em stationary} in the transtion from $u$ to $v$. If instead  $x P y$, then the size of $x$ in $u$ is strictly greater than that of $y$ in $v$, and 
we say that $x$ {\em progresses} to $y$ in the transition from $u$ to $v$. 
Finally, if $x$ and $y$ are unrelated by $S \cup P$, this
represents the lack of knowledge about the ordering of their sizes in $u$ and $v$, respectively.

\medskip
Transition graphs are a variant of the notion of {\em size-change graph} as defined in \cite[Def. 2.1]{Lee3}, abstractly representing the labeled transition system of the flow of values of certain variables through the calls of functions in a functional program, where labels convey the information needed to detect termination.
More precisely, a size-change graph $G:\texttt{f} \to \texttt{g}$ for a pair of function names $\texttt{f}$, 
$\texttt{g}$ out of some functional program $\texttt{p}$
is given by a triple $G = (\textit{Param}(\texttt{f}), \textit{Param}(\texttt{g}), \textsf{E})$, where $\textit{Param}(\texttt{f}), \textit{Param}(\texttt{g})$
are disjoint sets of variables occurring in the definitions of $\texttt{f}$ and $\texttt{g}$, and $\textsf{E} \subseteq \textit{Param}(\texttt{f}) \times \Set{\leqdownarrow, \downarrow} \times \textit{Param}(\texttt{g})$ and there are not two edges from $x \in \textit{Param}(\texttt{f})$ to
$y \in \textit{Param}(\texttt{g})$ labelled with both $\leqdownarrow$ and
$\downarrow$ in $\textsf{E}$.

In our view, $G:\texttt{f} \to \texttt{g}$, 
$G = (\textit{Param}(\texttt{f}), \textit{Param}(\texttt{g}), \textsf{E})$ corresponds 
to the edge $\edgeG{(S_G,P_G)}{f}{g}{\Graph}$ of a transition graph $\Graph$, and
$X$ is the set of all parameters occurring in the program $\texttt{p}$, 
and $S_G = \Set{(x,y) \mid (x, \leqdownarrow, y) \in \textsf{E}}$ and 
$P_G = \Set{(x,y) \mid (x, \downarrow, y) \in \textsf{E}}$ are binary relations over $X$.
The transition graph $\Graph$ has vertices $V=$ the set of all functions $\texttt{f}$
defined in  in the program $\texttt{p}$, and has set of
bi-partite relations $\biRel = \Set{(S_G,P_G) | G \mbox{ size-change graph}}$.

A {\em multipath} \cite[Def. 2.3]{Lee3} is the composition of finitely or infinitely many size-change graphs. 
The composition $GG'$ of $G:\texttt{f} \to \texttt{g}$ with $G':\texttt{g} \to \texttt{h}$
requires that the target function symbols of $G$ and the source function symbol of $G'$ coincide. 
In the case of our transition graphs, we have a unique graph $\Graph$, with finite set of parameters $X \supseteq
 \textit{Param}(\texttt{f}) \cup \textit{Param}(\texttt{g}) \cup \textit{Param}(\texttt{h})$,
and we express the composition $GG'$ by composing 
the the edges $\edgeG{(S_G,P_G)}{\texttt{f}}{\texttt{g}}{\Graph}$ and  
$\edgeG{(S_{G'},P_{G'})}{\texttt{g}}{\texttt{h}}{\Graph}$.
