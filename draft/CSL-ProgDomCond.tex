\section{Injectivity and Progress Domain Condition}
\label{sec:progressDomainCond}

We define injectivity over relations and prove some of its properties. In general, a relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$ is {\em injective} if for all $x,x' \in X$ and $y \in Y$, $xRy$ and $x'Ry$ implies $x = x'$. Equivalently, $R$ is injective if all the $R(x)$ for $x \in X$ are disjoint. Moreover, the composition of two injective relations is always injective.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:comInjective}
If both $R \subseteq X \times Y$ and $R' \subseteq Y \times Z$ are injective, then $RR' \subseteq X \times Z$ is injective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $xRR'z$ and $x'RR'z$ then there are $y,y' \in Y$ such that $xRy$ and $yR'z$, and $x'Ry'$ and $y'R'z$. By the injectivity of $R'$ we have $y = y'$,
hence $x = x'$ by the injectivity of $R$.
\end{proof}

We say that a bi-partite relation $(S, P)$ is {\em injective} if $|(S, P)| = S \cup P$ is such in the above sense. We can then extend the previous lemma in the case of injective bi-partite relations.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:biRelInjectiveComp}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
If both $(S,P)$ and $(S',P')$ are injective, then $(S,P) \comp (S',P')$ is injective.

\item
If $(S',P')$ is injective, then composition can be simplified by:
\[ (S,P) \comp (S',P') = (SS', SP' \cup PS' \cup PP') \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
By (\ref{eq:unionComp}) in Section \ref{sec:size-change},  we know that $|(S,P) \comp (S',P')| =  |(S,P)| |(S',P')|$ which is ordinary composition of the injective reations $S \cup P$ and $S' \cup P'$, hence it is injective by Lemma \ref{lem:comInjective}.

\item
By definition the stationary component of $(S,P) \comp (S',P')$ is $S'' \setminus P''$
where $S'' =S S'$ and $P'' = SS', SP' \cup PS' \cup PP'$. In order to prove that
$S'' \setminus P'' = S S'$ we have to prove that $S''$ and $P''$ are disjoint. 
We assume $xSyS'z$ and $x S \cup P y' S \cup P z$ (there is some other trace from $x$ to $y$) 
in order to prove that this trace is not in $P''$, namely that
$\neg (x P  y')$ and $\neg (y' P  z)$.  From $S' \cup P'$ injective and $y S' \cup P' z$
we deduce that $y = y'$. How thesis becomes $\neg (x P  y)$ and $\neg (y P  z)$.
This follow from $xSy$ and $yS'z$ and $S \cap P = \emptyset$ and 
$S' \cap P' = \emptyset$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}


We say that the set of bi-partite relations $\biRel$ is {\em injective} if all $(S,P) \in \biRel$ are such. By Lemma \ref{lem:biRelInjectiveComp}, if $\biRel$ is injective, then $\biRel^*$ is such. If $\Graph = (V, \biRel, E)$ with injective $\biRel$, by abuse of language we say that $\Graph$ is injective.

The notion of injectivity is crucial when analyzing the structure of transition graphs arising from pre-proofs in $\CLJIDomega$ and $\CLKIDomega$. In particular, the construction of the graph $\mathcal{G}_{\Pi}$ associated with a pre-proof $\Pi$ with contexts as lists, as defined in the previous sections, is always injective. 
This observation leads to the following result:

\begin{theorem}\label{thr:injective-pre-proofs}
Let $\Pi$ be any $\CLJIDomega$ or $\CLKIDomega$ pre-proof. Then $\Graph_\Pi$ is injective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    The construction of the bi-partite relations $(S,P)_{v,v'}$ specified in Definition \ref{def:preProofTrans} follows the trace relation. Thus, a case-by-case analysis of the sub-occurrence relation for each rule shows that all the arrows define injective relations. In all cases,
    the trace relation is defined by unions of injective relations having disjoint 
    codomains, hence injective.
\end{proof}

In the previous section, we have seen that the SCT condition for transition graphs and the GCT condition for cyclic proofs coincide up to rephrasing GTC in terms of transition graphs; henceforth, we shall use GTC and SCT as synonymous. We have also proved that the transition graphs associated to cyclic pre-proofs in systems $\CLJIDomega$ and $\CLKIDomega$ are injective. In this section, we formalize a new condition
on transition graphs, which we call {\em Progress Domain Condition}, shortly PDC, and show that, although it is strictly weaker than GCT, it implies GTC in the case of injective graphs. 

\begin{definition}[Progress Domain Condition - PDC]\label{def:PDC}
A transition $\Graph = (V,\biRel,E)$ 
satisfies the {\em Prograss Domain Condition} 
if for all self-composable $(S,P) \in \biRel^*$ there are $x, y \in  \dom{S \cup P}$ such that
$x$ progresses to $y$ we have:
\[\forall (S,P) \in \biRel^*, v \in V.\;\edgestar{(S,P)}{v}{v} \Then \exists x, y \in \dom{S \cup P}.\; x P y \]
\end{definition}



\begin{proposition}\label{prop:GTCimplPDC}
If $\Graph$ satisfies GTC, then it satisfies PDC.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\edgestar{(S,P)}{v}{v}$ be a self-composable path of $\Graph^*$. Then there is a cycle in $\Graph^*$ and an infinite path
\[\ppath : v \stackrel{(S,P)}{\longrightarrow} v \stackrel{(S,P)}{\longrightarrow} v \stackrel{(S,P)}{\longrightarrow}\cdots\]
where $(S,P) = (S_{0,c-1},P_{0,c-1})$ where $c > 0$ is the length of the cycle. By GTC, there is a trace $\tau = x_k, x_1, \ldots$ such that $\tau\models\ppath$ which is infinitely progressing
and $\tau$ must follow $\ppath$ since some index $k$. $\tau$
must progress in some finite subpaths $\edgestar{(S,P)}{v}{v}$.

By Proposition \ref{prop:progresing-and-P}, it follows that $x_a P x_b$ for some $k \leq a < b$; now $x_a, x_b \in \dom{S \cup P}$ since
$\tau\models \ppath$. It follows that $\Graph$ satisfies PDC.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:PDCnotGTC}
The inverse of Proposition \ref{prop:GTCimplPDC} does not hold. Consider $X_0 = \Set{x_1, x_2}$ and $\biRel_0 = \Set{(S_0,P_0)}$ where
$S_0 = \Set{(x_1, x_1)}$ and $P_0 = \Set{(x_2, x_1)}$. Let $\Graph_0 = (\Set{v}, \biRel_0, \Set{\edge{(S_0,P_0)}{v}{v}})$. Then $(S_0,P_0)$
is self-composing, $x_1, x_2 \in \dom{S_0 \cup P_0}$ and $x_2 P_0 x_1$, hence $\Graph_0$ satisfies the PDC.

On the other hand, the infinite path $\ppath_0$ represented in Figure \ref{fig:PDC-counterexample} is such that the only infinite traces that follow it are
$\tau_0 : x_1 , x_1, \cdots$ and $\tau_1 : x_2, x_1 , x_1, \cdots$, and neither of them is infinitely progressing. \input{CSL-PDC-figure}
\end{remark}

The key point in the counterexample of Remark \ref{rem:PDCnotGTC} is that $\biRel_0$ is not injective. Toward establishing that PDC implies GTC
in case of an injective $\biRel$, we first prove the lemma below for transitive relations in 
$\biRel$. 

We say that $(S,P)$ is {\em transitive} if $S \cup P$ is such, namely $x(S \cup P)y(S \cup P)z$ implies $x(S \cup P) z$, for all $x, y, z \in X$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:transitivity}
Suppose that $\Graph$ is injective and satisfies PDC.
If $(S,P)$ is self-composing in $\Graph$ 
and $(S,P)$ is transitive then there exists $x \in X$ such that $xPx$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the hypothesis that $\Graph$ satisfies PDC, there exist $x,y \in \dom{S \cup P}$ such that $xPy$. Since $P \subseteq S \cup P$ we also
have that $x(S \cup P)y$. On the other hand, since $y \in \dom{S \cup P}$, there is $z \in X$ such that $y(S \cup P)z$. From this, by transitivity, it follows that
$x(S \cup P)z$, hence $x = y$ since $(S,P)$ is injective. From $x=y$ and $xPy$ we conclude
that $xPx$.
\end{proof}

In the proof of Theorem \ref{thr:PDC+injImpliesGTC} below, we use the infinite Ramsey's Theorem. We preliminarily introduce some terminology.
Let $N \subseteq \Nat$, then $[N]^m = \Set{Y \subseteq N \mid |Y| = m}$ can be regarded as the complete (undirected) hypergraph where
all hyperedges $Y \subseteq N$ have the same cardinality $m$ (also called $m$-regular in the literature).
Taking $m = 2$, we have that $[N]^2 = \Set{\Set{x,y} \subseteq N \mid x \neq y}$ is the set of edges of the complete undirected graph whose vertices are in $N$. 

A {\em finite coloring} of $[N]^2$ is a map  
$c:[N]^2 \to \underline{k}$ for some $k > 0$, where $\underline{k} = \Set{1, \ldots , k}$, the set of ``colors'', is the set of the first $k$ positive integers.
A subset $[A]^2 \subseteq [N]^2$ is {\em monocromatic} w.r.t. the coloring $c$ if there is $i \in \underline{k}$ such that  $c(Y) = i$ for all
$(a,b) \in [A]^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Ramsey's Theorem]\label{thr:Ramsey}
For all finite coloring $c$ of $[\Nat]^2$ there exists $A \subseteq \Nat$, $A$ infinite, such that $[A]^2$ is monocromatic with respect to $c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof} See e.g. Theorem 5 in Chapter 1 of \cite{Graham1980}.
\end{proof}

By abuse of language, we say that $c:E \to \underline{k}$ is a finite coloring for any set $E$ of edges.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:Ramsey}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a complete undirected graph with infinite denumerable $V$, and let $c: E \to \underline{k}$ be a finite coloring of its edges,
then there exists an infinite $U \subseteq V$ that is a complete subgraph of $G$ and such that for all distinct $u, v \in H$ we have $c(\Set{u,v}) = i$ for some $i \in \underline{k}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
As $V$ is denumerable, the graphs $[\Nat]^2$ and $[V]^2$ are isomorphic.
Hence,  up to isomorphism, the corollary is a particular case of Theorem \ref{thr:Ramsey}.
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem]\label{thr:PDC+injImpliesGTC}
Let $\Graph = (V, \biRel, E)$ be a transition graph with injective $\biRel$; if $\Graph$ satisfies PDC, then it satisfies GTC.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \[\ppath : v_0 \stackrel{(S_0,P_0)}{\longrightarrow} v_1 \stackrel{(S_1,P_1)}{\longrightarrow} v_2 \stackrel{(S_2,P_2)}{\longrightarrow}\cdots\]
be an infinite path of $\Graph = (V, \biRel, E)$, where $v_i = \source{\ppath(i)}$ for $i < \omega$.
Also, $\ppath$ is a path of $\Graph^*$, where for all $0 \leq i < j < \omega$ there is an edge $\edgestar{(S_{i,j}, P_{i,j})}{v_i}{v_j}$, where
$(S_{i,j}, P_{i,j}) = (S_{i}, P_{i}) \comp \cdots \comp (S_{j-1}, P_{j-1})$.

Let $G = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ be the undirected graph formed by the sets
$\mathcal{V} = \Set{(i, v_i) \mid i < \omega}$, which is denumerable, and $\mathcal{E} = \Set{\Set{(i, v_i), (j, v_j)} \mid i < j}$; hence $G$ is complete.

Note that the edges in $\mathcal{E}$ correspond
to the edges $\edgestar{(S_{i,j}, P_{i,j})}{v_i}{v_j}$ of $\Graph^*$. Since all the $(S_{i,j}, P_{i,j}) \in \biRel^*$, which is a finite set, the mapping
$c\Set{(i, v_i), (j, v_j)} = (S_{i,j}, P_{i,j})$ is a finite coloring of $\mathcal{E}$.

By Corollary \ref{cor:Ramsey}, there is an infinite $\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{V}$ such that all $\Set{(i, v_i), (j, v_j)} \subseteq \mathcal{H}$
are edges in $\mathcal{E}$ (so $\mathcal{H}$ is a clique, namely a complete subgraph of $G$) with $c\Set{(i, v_i), (j, v_j)} = (S, P)$ for some
$(S, P) \in \biRel^*$.

As $V$ is finite, there exists $u \in V$ such that, for infinitely many $i < \omega$, $(i, u) \in \mathcal{H}$.
This determines a cofinal subpath $\ppath_{\mathcal{H}} : u \stackrel{(S,P)}{\longrightarrow} u \stackrel{(S,P)}{\longrightarrow} u \cdots$ of $\ppath$ where, $(S,P)$ is self-composing and, by the above, $(S,P)\comp (S,P) = (S,P)$, hence $(S,P)$ is transitive. By the hypotheses that $\Graph$ is
injective and satisfies PDC, Lemma \ref{lem:transitivity} implies that there exists $x \in X$ such that $x P x$.  
It remains to show that there is a trace $\tau$ such that $\tau \models \ppath$ and that, whenever $v_i = u$, $\tau(i) = x$.
Let $i_0 < j_0$ be the first two indexes such that $v_{i_0} = u = v_{j_0}$ and $(S,P) = (S_{i_0,j_0},P_{i_0,j_0})$; then in $\ppath$ we have the subpath:
\[u = v_{i_0} \stackrel{(S_{i_0},P_{i_0})}{\longrightarrow} \cdots \stackrel{(S_{j_0},P_{j_0})}{\longrightarrow} v_{j_0} = u \]
where we assume that the $v_j \neq u$ for all $i_0 < j < j_0$. 

Since $x P x$ and clearly $P \subseteq S \cup P = (S_{i_0} \cup P_{i_0}) \cdots (S_{j_0-1} \cup P_{j_0-1})$, there is a sequence $x = x_{i_0}, \ldots , x_{j_0} =x$ such that for all $i_{0} \leq i < j_0$, $x_i(S_{i} \cup P_{i}) x_{i+1}$ so that $x(S \cup P) x$.

Now, by repeating the same construction for all the paths $\edgestar{(S,P)}{u}{u}$ in $\ppath_{\mathcal{H}}$, we obtain a trace $\tau$ such that $\tau \models \ppath$, $\tau(i) = x$ for infinitely many $i$, which are progress points w.r.t. $P$. Hence $\Graph$ satisfies GTC by the arbitrary choice of $\ppath$.
\end{proof}
