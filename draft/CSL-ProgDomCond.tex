\section{Injectivity and Progress Domain Condition}
\label{sec:progressDomainCond}

We define injectivity over relations and prove some of its properties. Our goal is finding a case in which GTC is simpler to state. A relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$ is {\em injective} if for all $x,x' \in X$ and $y \in Y$, $xRy$ and $x'Ry$ implies $x = x'$. Equivalently, $R$ is injective if all the $R(x)$ for $x \in X$ are disjoint. Moreover, the composition of two injective relations is always injective.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:comInjective}
If both $R \subseteq X \times Y$ and $R' \subseteq Y \times Z$ are injective, then $RR' \subseteq X \times Z$ is injective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $xRR'z$ and $x'RR'z$ then there are $y,y' \in Y$ such that $xRy$ and $yR'z$, and $x'Ry'$ and $y'R'z$. By the injectivity of $R'$ we have $y = y'$,
hence $x = x'$ by the injectivity of $R$.
\end{proof}

We say that a bi-partite relation $(S, P)$ is {\em injective} if $|(S, P)| = S \cup P$ is such in the above sense. We can then extend the previous lemma in the case of injective bi-partite relations.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:biRelInjectiveComp}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
If both $(S,P)$ and $(S',P')$ are injective, then $(S,P) \comp (S',P')$ is injective.

\item
If $(S',P')$ is injective, then composition can be simplified by:
\[ (S,P) \comp (S',P') = (SS', SP' \cup PS' \cup PP') \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
By (\ref{eq:unionComp}) in Section \ref{sec:size-change},  we know that $|(S,P) \comp (S',P')| =  |(S,P)| |(S',P')|$ which is ordinary composition of the injective reations $S \cup P$ and $S' \cup P'$, hence it is injective by Lemma \ref{lem:comInjective}.

\item
By definition the stationary component of $(S,P) \comp (S',P')$ is $S'' \setminus P''$
where $S'' =S S'$ and $P'' = SS', SP' \cup PS' \cup PP'$. Our thesis becomes:
$S'' \setminus P'' = S S'$. We have to prove that $S''$ and $P''$ are disjoint. 
We assume $xSyS'z$ and $x (S \cup P) y' (S \cup P) z$ 
(there is some other trace from $x$ to $y$) 
in order to prove that this trace is not in $P''$, namely that
$\neg (x P  y')$ and $\neg (y' P  z)$.  From $S' \cup P'$ injective and $y (S' \cup P') z$
we deduce that $y = y'$. Our thesis is now $\neg (x P  y)$ and $\neg (y P  z)$.
This follow from $xSy$ and $yS'z$ and $S \cap P = S' \cap P' = \emptyset$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}


We say that the set of bi-partite relations $\biRel$ is {\em injective} if all $(S,P) \in \biRel$ are such. If $\Graph = (V, \biRel, E)$ with injective $\biRel$, by abuse of language we say that $\Graph$ is injective. By Lemma \ref{lem:biRelInjectiveComp}, if $\biRel$ is injective, then its composition closure $\biRel^*$ is such. 

The notion of injectivity is crucial when analyzing the structure of transition graphs arising from pre-proofs in $\CLJIDomega$ and $\CLKIDomega$. In particular, the construction of the graph $\mathcal{G}_{\Pi}$ associated with a pre-proof $\Pi$ with contexts as lists, as defined in the previous sections, is always injective. 
This observation leads to the following result:

\begin{theorem}\label{thr:injective-pre-proofs}
Let $\Pi$ be any $\CLJIDomega$ or $\CLKIDomega$ pre-proof. 
Then $\Graph_\Pi$ is injective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Prop. \ref{prop:injective-trace}.\ref{prop:injective-trace-02}.

In the previous section, we have seen that the SCT condition for transition graphs and the GCT condition for cyclic proofs coincide up to rephrasing GTC in terms of transition graphs. %; henceforth, we shall use GTC and SCT as synonymous. 
We have also proved that the transition graphs associated with cyclic pre-proofs in systems $\CLJIDomega$ and $\CLKIDomega$ are injective. In this section, we formalize a new condition
on transition graphs, which we call {\em Progress Domain Condition}, shortly PDC, and show that, although it is strictly weaker than SCT, it implies SCT in the case of injective graphs. Hence PDC is a simpler reformulation of GTC for our deduction system.

\begin{definition}[Progress Domain Condition - PDC]\label{def:PDC}
A transition $\Graph = (V,\biRel,E)$ 
satisfies the {\em Progress Domain Condition} 
if for all self-composable $(S,P) \in \biRel^*$ there are $x, y \in  \dom{S \cup P}$ such that
$x$ progresses to $y$. In other words we require:
\[\forall (S,P) \in \biRel^*, v \in V.\; (\edgestar{(S,P)}{v}{v}) \Then \exists x, y \in \dom{S \cup P}.\; x P y \]
\end{definition}



\begin{proposition}\label{prop:GTCimplPDC}
If $\Graph$ satisfies SCT, then it satisfies PDC.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\edgestar{(S,P)}{v}{v}$ be a self-composable path of $\Graph^*$. 
Then there is an infinite path
\[\ppath : v \stackrel{(S,P)}{\longrightarrow} v \stackrel{(S,P)}{\longrightarrow} v \stackrel{(S,P)}{\longrightarrow}\cdots\]
%where $(S,P) = (S_{0,c-1},P_{0,c-1})$ where $c > 0$ is the length of the cycle. 
By SCT, there is a trace $\tau = x_k, x_{k+1}, \ldots$ such that $\tau\models\ppath$ 
and $\tau$ is infinitely progressing
%and $\tau$ must follow $\ppath$ since some index $k$. 
$\tau$ must progress in some step $\edgestar{(S,P)}{v}{v}$.

By Proposition \ref{prop:progresing-and-P}, it follows that $x_a P x_{a+1}$ for some 
$a \ge k$; now $x_a, x_{a+1} \in \dom{S \cup P}$ since
$\tau\models \ppath$. It follows that $\Graph$ satisfies PDC.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:PDCnotGTC}
The inverse of Proposition \ref{prop:GTCimplPDC} does not hold without injectivity. 
Consider $X_0 = \Set{x_1, x_2}$ and $\biRel_0 = \Set{(S_0,P_0)}$ where
$S_0 = \Set{(x_1, x_1)}$ and $P_0 = \Set{(x_2, x_1)}$. Let $\Graph_0 = (\Set{v}, \biRel_0, \Set{\edge{(S_0,P_0)}{v}{v}})$. Then $(S_0,P_0)$
is self-composing, $x_1, x_2 \in \dom{S_0 \cup P_0}$ and $x_2 P_0 x_1$, hence $\Graph_0$ satisfies the PDC.

On the other hand, the infinite path $\ppath_0$ represented in Figure \ref{fig:PDC-counterexample} is such that the only infinite traces that follow it are
$\tau_0 : x_1 , x_1, x_1, x_1, \cdots$ and $\tau_1 : x_2, x_1 , x_1, x_1, \cdots$, 
and neither of them is infinitely progressing. \input{CSL-PDC-figure}
\end{remark}

The key point in the counterexample of Remark \ref{rem:PDCnotGTC} is that $\biRel_0$ is not injective, both $x_1$ and $x_2$ are related with $x_1$. 
Toward establishing that PDC implies SCT
in case of an injective $\biRel$, we first prove the lemma below for transitive relations in 
$\biRel$. 

We say that $(S,P)$ is {\em transitive} if $S \cup P$ is such, namely $x(S \cup P)y(S \cup P)z$ implies $x(S \cup P) z$, for all $x, y, z \in X$. We first prove that PDC implies the so-called
\quotationMarks{algorithmic criterion for SCT} (\cite{Lee1},Section 3.2).

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:transitivity}
Suppose that $\Graph$ is injective and satisfies PDC, and that 
$(S,P)$ is self-composing in $\Graph$ 
\begin{enumerate}
\item
\label{lem:transitivity-01}
If $(S,P)$ is transitive then there exists $x \in X$ such that $xPx$.

\item
\label{lem:transitivity-02}
\emph{(SCT criterion)} If $(S,P)(S,P)=(S,P)$ then there exists $x \in X$ such that $xPx$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
\item
By the hypothesis that $\Graph$ satisfies PDC, there exist $x,y \in \dom{S \cup P}$ such that $xPy$ and with more reason $x(S \cup P)y$. On the other hand, since $y \in \dom{S \cup P}$, there is $z \in X$ such that $y(S \cup P)z$. From this, by transitivity, it follows that
$x(S \cup P)z$, hence $x = y$ since $(S,P)$ is injective. From $x=y$ and $xPy$ we conclude
that $xPx$.
\item
$(S,P)(S,P)=(S,P)$ implies that $(S,P)$ is transitive. Now we apply the previous point.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

Using Theorem 4 of \cite{Lee1} from PDC we may already deduce the SCT criterion,
then conclude SCT. We prefer to include a 
more direct proof, %. In the proof of Theorem \ref{thr:PDC+injImpliesGTC} below, we use 
using the infinite Ramsey's Theorem. We preliminarily introduce some terminology for it.

%Let $N \subseteq \Nat$, then $[N]^m = \Set{Y \subseteq N \mid |Y| = m}$ can be regarded as the complete (undirected) hypergraph where

$[N]^2 = \Set{\Set{x,y} \subseteq N \mid x \neq y}$ is the set of edges of the complete undirected graph whose vertices are in $N$. 

A {\em finite coloring} of $[N]^2$ is a map  
$c:[N]^2 \to \underline{k}$ for some $k > 0$, where $\underline{k} = \Set{1, \ldots , k}$, the set of ``colors'', is the set of the first $k$ positive integers.
A subset $[A]^2 \subseteq [N]^2$ is {\em monocromatic} w.r.t. the coloring $c$ if there is $i \in \underline{k}$ such that  $c((a,b)) = i$ for all
$(a,b) \in [A]^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Ramsey's Theorem]\label{thr:Ramsey}
For all finite coloring $c$ of $[\Nat]^2$ there exists $A \subseteq \Nat$, $A$ infinite, such that $[A]^2$ is monocromatic with respect to $c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof} See e.g. Theorem 5 in Chapter 1 of \cite{Graham1980}.
\end{proof}

By abuse of language, we say that $c:E \to \underline{k}$ is a finite coloring for any set $E$ of edges.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:Ramsey}
Let $G = (V, E)$ be a complete undirected graph with infinite denumerable $V$, and let $c: E \to \underline{k}$ be a finite coloring of its edges,
then there exists an infinite $U \subseteq V$ that is a complete subgraph of $G$ and such that for all distinct $u, v \in H$ we have $c(\Set{u,v}) = i$ for some $i \in \underline{k}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
As $V$ is denumerable, the graphs $[\Nat]^2$ and $[V]^2$ are isomorphic.
Hence,  up to isomorphism, the corollary is a particular case of Theorem \ref{thr:Ramsey}.
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem]\label{thr:PDC+injImpliesGTC}
Let $\Graph = (V, \biRel, E)$ be a transition graph with injective $\biRel$; if $\Graph$ satisfies PDC, then it satisfies SCT.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\ppath : v_0 \stackrel{(S_0,P_0)}{\longrightarrow} v_1 \stackrel{(S_1,P_1)}{\longrightarrow} v_2 \stackrel{(S_2,P_2)}{\longrightarrow}\cdots$
be any infinite path of $\Graph^* = (V, \biRel^*, E^*)$, where $v_i = \source{\ppath(i)}$ for $i < \omega$.
%Also, $\ppath$ is a path of $\Graph^*$, where 
For all $0 \leq i < j < \omega$ there is an edge $\edgestar{(S_{i,j}, P_{i,j})}{v_i}{v_j}$, 
defined by
$(S_{i,j}, P_{i,j}) = (S_{i}, P_{i}) \comp \cdots \comp (S_{j-1}, P_{j-1})$.

Let $G = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ be the undirected graph formed by the sets
$\mathcal{V} = \Set{(i, v_i) \mid i < \omega}$, which is denumerable, and $\mathcal{E} = \Set{\Set{(i, v_i), (j, v_j)} \mid i < j}$; hence $G$ is a complete graph.

The edges in $\mathcal{E}$ correspond
to the edges $\edgestar{(S_{i,j}, P_{i,j})}{v_i}{v_j}$ of $\Graph^*$. 
Since $(S_{i,j}, P_{i,j}) \in \biRel^*$, which is a finite set, the mapping
$c\Set{(i, v_i), (j, v_j)} = (S_{i,j}, P_{i,j})$ is a finite coloring of $\mathcal{E}$.

By Corollary \ref{cor:Ramsey}, there is an infinite $\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{V}$ such that for some $(S, P) \in \biRel^*$ and for all $i, j \in \mathcal{H}$, $i \not  = j$ we have
%all $\Set{(i, v_i), (j, v_j)} \subseteq \mathcal{H}$
%are edges in $\mathcal{E}$ (so $\mathcal{H}$ is a clique, namely a complete subgraph of $G$) with 
$c\Set{(i, v_i), (j, v_j)} = (S, P)$. 

As $V$ is finite, there exists $u \in V$ such that for infinitely many $i < \omega$
we have $(i, u) \in \mathcal{H}$.
This determines a cofinal subpath $\ppath_{\mathcal{H}} : u \stackrel{(S,P)}{\longrightarrow} u \stackrel{(S,P)}{\longrightarrow} u \cdots$ of $\ppath$ where $(S,P)$ is self-composing and, by the above, the \quotationMarks{colors} $(S,P)\comp (S,P)$ and $ (S,P)$ are the same.
%, hence $(S,P)$ is transitive. 
By the hypotheses that $\Graph$ is
injective and satisfies PDC, Lemma \ref{lem:transitivity}.\ref{lem:transitivity-02} 
implies that there exists $x \in X$ such that $x P x$.  
It remains to show that there is a trace $\tau$ such that $\tau \models \ppath$ and that, whenever $v_i = u$, $\tau(i) = x$.
Let $i_0 < i_1$ be the first two indexes such that $v_{i_0} = u = v_{i_1}$ and $(S,P) = (S_{i_0,i_1},P_{i_0,i_1})$; then in $\ppath$ we have the subpath:
\[u = v_{i_0} \stackrel{(S_{i_0},P_{i_0})}{\longrightarrow} \cdots \stackrel{(S_{i_1},P_{i_1})}{\longrightarrow} v_{i_1} = u \]
where we assume that the $v_j \neq u$ for all $i_0 < j < i_1$. 

Since $x P x$ and clearly $P \subseteq S \cup P = (S_{i_0} \cup P_{i_0}) \cdots (S_{i_1-1} \cup P_{i_1-1})$, there is a sequence $x = x_{i_0}, \ldots , x_{i_1} =x$ such that for all $i_{0} \leq i < i_1$, $x_i(S_{i} \cup P_{i}) x_{i+1}$ %so that $x(S \cup P) x$.
and $x_i P_{i} x_{i+1}$ for some $i_{0} \leq i < i_1$.
Assume that $i_0 < i_1 < i_2 < \ldots$ are the indexes such that 
$v_{i_0} = v_{i_1} = v_{i_2} = \ldots = u$.
Now, by repeating the same construction for all consecutive indexes $i_k, i_{k+1}$ in the
sub-sequence, 
% the paths $\edgestar{(S,P)}{u}{u}$ in $\ppath_{\mathcal{H}}$, 
 we obtain a trace $\tau$ such that $\tau \models \ppath$, $\tau(i) = x$ for infinitely many $i$, 
 including infinitely many progress points w.r.t. $P$. 
 Hence $\Graph$ satisfies SCT by the arbitrary choice of $\ppath$.
\end{proof}
