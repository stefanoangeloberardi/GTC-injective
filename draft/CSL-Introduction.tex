\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:Introduction}



Cyclic proof systems are a particular way of reasoning with inductively defined predicates, where derivations using induction rules are replaced by infinite regular proof trees, whose infinite branches satisfy the ``infinite descent'' condition. 
Following Fermat's method, if such a branch exists in a proof using the definition of inductive predicates infinitely many times, then an infinite sequence of decreasing-size elements satisfying such predicates should exist, contradicting their well-founded definition. As a consequence, cyclic proofs encode an underlying inductive argument that is sound.

%In this paper, by \quotationMarks{regular tree} we mean 
%\quotationMarks{a possibly infinite tree having finitely many distinct sub-trees}.
The {\em global trace condition} (GTC) for cyclic proofs is a sufficient {\em correctness}
condition for a regular proof-tree for sequent calculus introduced by Brotherston and Simpson
(\cite{Brotherston1, Brotherston2,BrotherstonSimpson}). GTC says that
in all infinite branches of the proof-tree, we can find some inductively defined 
formal parameter whose value is decreasing in size infinitely many times in the branch. 
This condition is called \emph{infinite descent} in Brotherston PhD thesis,
and it implies the correctness of the 
conclusion of the proof-tree (\cite{Brotherston1}, Proposition 4.2.10). 
It may be counter-intuitive, but a good strategy 
to look for a finite proof is to look for a regular and \emph{infinite} proof 
satisfying GTC first.
Indeed, the use of GTC combines well both with proof search and with program synthesis
on realistic examples (\cite{10.1007/978-3-642-35182-2_25,10.1145/3632888, 10.1007/978-3-031-90643-5_18,  10.1145/3453483.3454087,  10.1145/3018610.3018623, Stratulat_2021, Brotherston2020}). 

On the other hand, C.S. Lee, N. Jones, A. Ben-Amram
introduced the \emph{size-change termination} condition (SCT) 
to provide a sufficient condition for totality of partial recursive maps on natural numbers
(\cite{Lee1}). A priori, SCT and GTC have nothing to do each other, but then
I. Mirai remarked that the correctness condition GTC for cyclic proofs 
can be seen as a particular case of SCT for partial recursive maps (\cite{Mirai}). 

In this paper we go farther establishing relations between cyclic proofs and SCT. 
C.S. Lee and A. Ben-Amram considered several sub-cases for the SCT condition, including
SCT for fan-in-free graphs, namely, for graphs in which no nodes has two incoming edges
(\cite{Lee2} and \cite{Lee3}, Section 2.4. \quotationMarks{Subclasses of SCT}). 
We want to study cyclic proofs associated to fan-in-free graphs. 

There are several advantages
in considering this restricted class. GTC can be expressed an essentially weaker condition
in the case of fan-in free graphs, and it can be checked faster. 
In a future work, we hope to show that we can show the correctness of cyclic proofs 
satisfying GTC can be obtained by a simpler proof in the case of fan-in free graphs.

The original version of cyclic proofs by Brotherston and Simpson, named $\CLKIDomega$, 
is \emph{not} fan-in free. As a counterexample, consider the following instance of left-equality 
rule, using sequents as sets, where $N\mathbf{t}$ means: 
\quotationMarks{$\mathbf{t}$ is an inductively defined natural number}.

 \begin{center}
    %EQUALITY LEFT
    %-----------------------------------------
    \begin{prooftree}
        \hypo{ \Gamma, \vnode1{N\mathbf{t}} \vdash \Delta}
        \infer1[(=L)]{\Gamma, t = u, \vnode2{N\mathbf{t}}, \vnode3{N\mathbf{u}} \vdash \Delta} 
    \end{prooftree}

    % DRAW EDGES
    $
    \dirflowblueedges{node3/node1, node2/node1} 
    $
  \end{center}
  
If we apply Brotherston's definition for trace (details can be found in 
\cite{Brotherston1}, Definition 4.2.3), we obtain that the inductive formula $N\mathbf{t}$ 
in the premise has at least two incoming edges obtained from the trace relation, from the inductive formulas $N\mathbf{t}$ and $N\mathbf{u}$
in the conclusion. Even considering sequents as multisets, fan-in free connections
between inductive formulas are not guaranteed. For example, consider the left-weakening rule:
    
    \begin{center}
      \begin{prooftree}
        \hypo{\Gamma, \vnode1{N\mathbf{u}} \vdash \Delta}
        \infer1[(WeakL)]{\Gamma, \vnode2{N\mathbf{u}}, \vnode3{N\mathbf{u}} \vdash \Delta} 
      \end{prooftree}

      % DRAW EDGES
    $
    \dirflowblueedges{node3/node1, node2/node1} 
    $  
    \end{center}

where one of the inductive predicates $N\mathbf{u}$ in the conclusion is a direct descendant of the inductive predicate in the premise, while the other was freshly introduced by the weakening rule.

In this paper we introduce a variant of Brotherston's cyclic proofs in which connections
between atomic inductive formulas, given by the trace relation, are fan-in free. To this aim, we represent left and right
hand side of a sequent with a list of formulas, and trace \emph{occurrences} instead of formulas. We will check that the set of theorem we can derive does not change, while the algorithm
checking GTC has a simpler formulation in this case.
