\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:Introduction}

In this paper by \quotationMarks{regular tree} we mean 
\quotationMarks{a possibly infinite tree having finitely many distinct sub-trees}.
The {\em global trace condition} (GTC) for cyclic proofs is a sufficient {\em correctness}
condition for a regular proof-tree for sequent calculus
(\cite{Brotherston1, Brotherston2}). GTC says that
in all infinite branches of the proof-tree we can find some finite-size inductively defined 
formal parameter which is decreasing in size infinitely many times in the branch. 
This condition is called {\em infinite descent } and it implies the correctness of the 
conclusion of the proof-tree (\cite{Brotherston1}, Proposition 4.2.10). 
It may be counter-intuitive, but a good strategy 
to look for a finite proof is to look for a regular and possibly infinite proof first.
Indeed, GTC combines well both with proof search and with program synthesis
on realistic examples (\cite{10.1007/978-3-642-35182-2_25,10.1145/3632888, 10.1007/978-3-031-90643-5_18,  10.1145/3453483.3454087,  10.1145/3018610.3018623, Stratulat_2021, Brotherston2020}).

The {\em size-change termination} condition, SCT 
In Mirai remarks that GTC can be seen as a particular case of the {\em size-change termination} condition, SCT, restricted to fan-in free graphs. 
We express cyclic proofs through a variant of size-change graphs, called transition graphs, whose edges are labelled by (finite) bi-partite relations, where the fan-in freeness corresponds to the injectivity of these relations. 


We show that injective transition graphs suffice to encode cyclic pre-proofs for a variant of Brotherston and Simpson's sequent calculus with inductive definitions;
then we prove that an essentially weaker condition implies GTC in this case.
